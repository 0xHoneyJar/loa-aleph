I clarify the adopted F-03 accepted-worker-return production-reachability design for cumulative run format 1.9.0-provisional and capability orchestrator-work-transitions as follows.

An original `claim-candidate:<index>` that passes the existing context-bound producer and semantic-return validation, but whose valid semantic declaration and complete `material_use` establish `CANNOT_DETERMINE` rather than affirmative semantic/material support, MUST retain its original selector identity and MUST receive exhaustive L2S accounting. For cumulative 1.9.0-provisional runs using orchestrator-work-transitions, Core shall provide a dedicated retained nonaffirmative claim-candidate L2S binding for this case.

The dedicated binding may be named `indeterminate-claim` or an equivalently explicit Core identifier, but its semantics are fixed by this clarification.

The retained selector remains the original:

`claim-candidate:<index>`

The producer-binding identity MUST continue to bind that exact selector and original accepted producer bytes.

The dedicated nonaffirmative claim binding MUST mechanically retain the original validated claim-candidate fields required to distinguish the proposal, including:

- the original output selector and output index;
- the reserved claim identifier associated with the proposal;
- the producer-returned tentative `normalized_claim` text;
- the complete ordered packet basis;
- the mechanically derived source basis;
- the producer-returned claim type;
- the complete validated semantic declaration;
- the complete ordered `material_use`;
- relevant validated source/packet/origin context already required by the existing semantic subject contract.

No field may be silently rewritten merely because the candidate is nonaffirmative.

The tentative `normalized_claim` retained in this binding is proposal text only. For this nonaffirmative binding it is NOT a canonical affirmative proposition and its retention does not assert that the source entails it.

The reserved claim identifier is a retained proposal reservation/reference only. It does not constitute a canonical CC definition, does not create a row in the canonical claim inventory, does not establish lineage-current claim identity, does not permit another canonical artifact to cite it as an admitted CC, and does not authorize a canonical representation-use receipt with `subject_kind = CC`.

This clarification grants no independent later-admission or reservation-reuse rule. Any later repaired or affirmative claim proposal must satisfy the separately applicable existing Core producer, semantic, material, review, reservation and admission rules before a canonical CC may exist.

An L2S verdict on this dedicated binding does not itself create or admit a canonical CC.

A `refuted` verdict retains the failure and follows the existing Core challenge/revision or unresolved path; the reviewer does not author a replacement claim.

A `cannot-determine` verdict retains the candidate and missing basis and follows the existing fresh-review/unresolved requirements. It does not authorize affirmative use.

No L2S result may silently convert this binding into an affirmative `claim` binding or canonical CC.

## Material preview

The material context shown to L2S for this dedicated nonaffirmative binding is a review preview, not a canonical representation-use receipt.

Core MUST derive this preview mechanically from the candidate's complete validated `MaterialUseInput`.

For `use_state = CANNOT_DETERMINE`, the preview MUST preserve the complete ordered `requirements[]`, including all existing OBJ identifiers, requested features, binding IDs, limitation references, fidelity declaration and reason.

- choose one preferred OBJ;
